01

First, the naming

The cluster work filed this building as “Littleton.” The CMS 2567 resolves it: Cherrelyn Healthcare Center, 5555 S Elati St, Littleton, Colorado 80120, provider number 020428. Same building, two names — the plan was indexed by city, the diligence by facility.

That matters beyond bookkeeping. The therapy caseload places Cherrelyn in a seven-building group — Cedars, Cherrelyn, LaVilla, Mantey, Skyline, Springs Village, Willow Tree — which is not the three-building cluster the earlier plan was written against. Cherrelyn carries 24 skilled and 38 long-term-care patients on the therapy caseload, the largest skilled load of the seven. Before this plan is presented, confirm which peer set it is being compared to.

The 50% skilled-mix target

Cherrelyn has never been above 13.6% skilled in forty months of census data. A 50% target is not a stretch goal against that record — it is a different denominator. The likeliest reading is the therapy caseload, where 24 of 62 patients (38.7%) are skilled.

This is the same defect the original brief caught in the agency and turnover lines: a number stated without its unit. Fix the denominator before the plan is presented, or the building will be measured against a target it cannot reach.

Read the two trends together

Skilled recovery and private-pay erosion are pulling in opposite directions on the same census. Since Jan 2026 the building has added roughly six points of skilled mix — worth more per day than anything else in this plan — while private pay quietly gave up three points over three years. Protecting the first and arresting the second are separate jobs with separate owners.

04

Six workstreams

Ordered by urgency, not by size. The first is time-boxed by a regulator; the next three are the margin.

1
Survey readiness Window open now

The last recertification survey closed 30 Jan 2025. Standard recert intervals run 9–15 months; Cherrelyn is at 19. A survey team can arrive any morning, and the plan should assume it happens inside the 100 days rather than after them.

Five tags were cited — F679 activities, F685 vision devices, F693 tube feeding, F849 hospice care plans, all at scope/severity D, plus F880 infection prevention at E, the only pattern-level finding. F880 is the exposure: it cited staff not wearing gowns and gloves for a resident on enhanced barrier precautions, and residents not offered hand hygiene before meals. Both are observable in the first hour of any survey.

The accepted plan of correction committed to DON audits weekly × 3 then monthly × 2. That cycle ended in mid-2025. Reinstate it now, and treat the four D-tags as a mock-survey checklist rather than closed items — F849 in particular was a documentation failure (hospice plan of care not integrated into the facility care plan) that recurs quietly.

Owner NHA + DON Day 0–30 Target Mock survey complete by day 21 Prior tags F679 F685 F693 F849 F880
2
Medicaid case-mix capture

The October 2025 CO135 listing carries 162 residents at an average index of 1.7143; the 151 Medicaid residents average 1.7001. Thirty-six residents — 22.2% — sit below a 1.20 index, clustered in four codes: V (1.19, n=24), X (1.10, n=7), S (0.96, n=3) and R (1.01, n=2).

With Medicaid at 93% of the listing and roughly 4,417 Medicaid days a month, the case-mix index is the largest single reimbursement lever in this plan. Assessment timing is not the problem — mean assessment age is 44 days and nothing on the listing exceeds 90. The question is capture: whether ADL and Section GG scoring, restorative nursing programs, depression and behavior coding reflect the care actually delivered to those 36 residents.

Audit that low-index cohort resident by resident against the chart. Where documentation supports a higher classification, the correction flows through the next assessment cycle; where it does not, the finding is a care-planning gap worth closing on its own terms.

Owner DON + MDS coordinator Day 0–60 Target +0.05 index on the Medicaid cohort Cohort 36 residents under 1.20
3
Hold the skilled recovery

Skilled mix ran 6.5% through 2025 and bottomed at 4.4% in January. It is 12.3% year to date in 2026, peaking at 13.6% in February — Medicare daily census went from 2.4 to 16.3 in five months. This is the single best trend in the building and it is only three months old.

Three months is not yet a system. Find out what changed — a referral relationship, a discharge planner, a marketing hire, a hospital contract — and write it down before the person who did it moves on. AdventHealth Littleton and AdventHealth Porter both appear in the vendor ledger and are the obvious referral sources to formalize.

Owner Admissions + NHA Day 0–45 Target Hold ≥12% through the plan window Baseline 12.3% YTD
4
Arrest private-pay erosion

Private-pay days fell from 400 a month in 2023 to 320 in 2025, and 264 year to date — a 19.9% decline over two full years with no month that breaks the trend. As a share of census it went 7.99% → 4.94%.

This is the quietest number in the file because occupancy hid it: the building backfilled every lost private day with a Medicaid day and the census line never moved. Diagnose it before setting a target — whether it is referral-source drift, a rate position that no longer competes locally, or private residents converting to Medicaid faster than they used to.

Owner NHA + business office Day 30–100 Target Stabilize at ≥300 days/mo Trend −80 days/mo vs 2023
5
Workforce Carried — unverified

The original plan’s strongest section, and the one this diligence set cannot touch. It commits to turnover from 61% to under 30% and agency from $17 to under $2 per patient day. No HR, payroll or agency data was provided, so both numbers remain as stated, unconfirmed.

Keep the workstream and its owner. Before presenting, resolve the unit mismatch the earlier brief caught — agency stated as “<$2 WPPD” in the narrative and “<$2,000/month” in the metrics table are different commitments by a factor of about eighty.

05

How the hundred days run

The sequence is forced by the survey window: everything a surveyor could observe happens first.

Day 0 – 30

Survey posture and the baseline

  • Reinstate the F880 audit cycle — enhanced barrier precautions and pre-meal hand hygiene, DON audit weekly
  • Full mock survey against all five prior tags, complete by day 21
  • Pull the 36 low-index residents and open the chart audit
  • Fix the skilled-mix denominator and restate the target
  • Request the four missing data sets in section 06
Day 31 – 60

Capture and controls

  • Case-mix corrections flow through the assessment cycle
  • Document what drove the skilled recovery; formalize the referral relationships behind it
  • Private-pay diagnosis complete — cause named before a target is set
  • Vendor dedupe and EFT conversion
  • Workforce numbers verified against payroll, or formally parked
Day 61 – 100

Hold and prove

  • Second CO135 listing compared against the October baseline
  • Skilled mix held at or above 12% for a fourth and fifth month
  • Private-pay intervention live with a measurable rate
  • Survey readiness maintained, not relaxed — the window stays open
  • Plan restated with every carried number either verified or dropped
06

What is still missing

Four gaps keep parts of this plan unverified. Each is one request away.

Cash collections detail The 100% target and 94% six-month average carry over from the original plan with nothing in this set to confirm them. Needs A/R aging and monthly collections against billed.
HR and payroll Turnover of 61% and the open-position count are unverified. Needs a termination report and current requisitions.
Agency invoices Both the $17 WPPD baseline and the target unit are unconfirmed. Needs twelve months of agency billing.
Colorado nursing-component rate Without it the case-mix opportunity can only be stated in index points, not dollars. A +0.05 index move is a 2.9% uplift on the nursing component — the dollar value needs the rate per index point and cannot be estimated from these documents.
On the numbers in this plan

Everything in sections 02 through 04 is computed directly from the five source documents and is reproducible from them. Nothing has been estimated, benchmarked against outside data, or carried forward without being marked as carried. Where a figure could not be derived — most importantly the dollar value of the case-mix opportunity — it is left unstated rather than modeled.
