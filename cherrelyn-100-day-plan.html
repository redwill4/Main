02

The Day-1 decision, repriced

Stopping Part B respiratory billing is the right compliance call. The P&L changes what it costs.

The model prices the exit off the trailing twelve months: 4543-0040 billed $1,742,597 gross, and at a 42.2% realization that is $735,376 of net revenue forgone. Both figures check out against the general ledger — blended Part B realization is stable at 42.1–42.3% across both halves of the year, so the rate is sound.

The basis is not. That line is accelerating hard.

What the exit actually costs
$1,324,552 net revenue forgone, at the Jan–Feb run-rate

Against the model’s $735,376. The exit is $589,176 more expensive than carried — a gap larger than the entire overtime lever. Annualizing the last three months rather than two still lands at $1,141,679.

A line that triples in five months is also the pattern that draws a Medicare probe, so the acceleration argues for the exit and raises its cost at the same time. That is the honest framing for the room: the decision gets easier and the arithmetic gets harder.

One scope question follows. Respiratory is 59% of the Part B book, not all of it — PT, OT and speech bill another $1,198,390 gross, worth about $505,700 net at the same realization. If the compliance concern is respiratory specifically, that revenue stays. If it is Part B billing generally, the exposure is roughly 1.7× what this plan carries. Settle that before Day 1 rather than after.

The number to walk in with

At the true run-rate, offsetting the Part B exit on skilled census alone takes 11.3 additional skilled residents per day. The building has already won 8.6 — a genuinely strong result that, under the original TTM math, would have covered the exit nearly twice over.

It no longer does. The gap is 2.7 ADC, about $258,000 a year. That gap is what overtime and case-mix capture exist to close, and it is why this plan cannot rely on a single lever.

04

Four levers, priced

Each carries a dollar value, an owner, and the account it comes out of.

1
Exit Part B respiratory Day 1

Stop billing 4543-0040 at close. Medically necessary respiratory care continues for Part A and skilled residents, bundled under PDPM — that is $599,491 of gross respiratory revenue on the Part A line that is unaffected because it was never separately collected.

Wind down respiratory therapy labor against the 72% of respiratory revenue that Part B represented. Retained staff serve the skilled census, which is growing. Budget the wind-down cost — severance is not in this bridge.

2
Hold the skilled census

Skilled ADC ran 13.4 across the P&L year and is 22.0 in 2026 Q1 — Medicare A room and board went from $23,170 in November to $188,405 in February. The recovery is real, recent, and already past the Upside assumption in the original model.

Which makes this a retention job, not a growth job. Find what changed — a referral relationship, a discharge planner, a hospital contract — and institutionalize it before the person who did it leaves. Every ADC held is worth about $94,900 a year at model assumptions.

3
Overtime 5.8× bigger than the plan said

The original plan named director overtime of $234,288. That is real — and it is 16% of the problem. Building-wide overtime is $1,355,761, which is 11.5% of worked payroll; the nursing subset is 15.6%. Both percentages match what the plan stated, but the dollar base was never shown.

CNA overtime alone is $452,339, a third of the total and the largest single line in the building. The recoverable premium across all departments is roughly $452,000, so the Base case asks for a little over half of it.

There is no agency or contract-nursing account anywhere in the ledger. Overtime is how this building flexes — which means schedule rebuilding and vacancy filling are the only levers, and cutting overtime without filling positions will simply reappear as agency spend under new ownership.

Owner DON + HR Day 0–60 EBITDAR $150K – $350K Base $1.36M across 24 accounts
4
Medicaid case-mix capture New lever

Not in the original model. The October CO135 listing shows 162 residents at a 1.7143 average index, with 36 of them — 22.2% — below a 1.20 index. Against 54,326 Medicaid days a year, index is the largest rate lever in the building.

MDS RN labor is $12,399 for the year against a $20M+ Medicaid book. Assessment timing is not the issue — mean assessment age is 44 days and nothing exceeds 90. Capture is: whether ADL and Section GG scoring, restorative programs and behavior coding reflect the care actually delivered.

The $157K in the Base case is an illustration at +0.05 index, not a commitment. It needs the Colorado nursing-component rate to firm up, and that rate is not in the diligence set.

Reconcile before presenting

The facility profile reports turnover of 37.4%, below the 44.4% national average. The prior 100-day plan reports 61%. Those cannot both be true, and the overtime case rests on which one is. A building at 37% turnover with $1.36M of overtime has a scheduling problem; a building at 61% has a retention problem, and they take different money and different time to fix.

06

The hundred days

Sequenced by cash impact. The Part B hole opens on Day 1, so the offsets cannot wait for Day 60.

Day 0 – 30

Stop the billing, protect the census

  • Part B respiratory billing stops at close; scope decision on therapy Part B made in writing
  • Respiratory therapy wind-down plan with severance costed — not in this bridge
  • Document what drove the skilled recovery and lock the referral relationships behind it
  • Reinstate the F880 audit cadence; mock survey by day 21
  • Confirm real Medicare and Medicaid per diems — the $400 differential is the bridge’s biggest unverified input
  • Reconcile the turnover conflict against payroll records
Day 31 – 60

Close the 2.7 ADC gap

  • Schedule rebuild reaching the CNA line, not just directors — the $452K sits there
  • Fill the vacancies driving overtime before cutting the overtime
  • Chart audit on the 36 residents below a 1.20 index; resource the MDS function
  • Private-pay diagnosis — name the cause before setting a target
  • Medicaid-pending backlog worked against the $443K bad-debt run rate
  • Re-point the $225K admin bonus pool at overtime and skilled-mix targets
Day 61 – 100

Prove the run-rate

  • Three clean months of post-exit P&L — the only real test of this bridge
  • Second CO135 listing compared against the October baseline
  • Skilled ADC held at 22 for a sixth consecutive month
  • Overtime tracked weekly against the $1.36M base, by department
  • FY budget built on actual post-exit run-rate, with rent and debt service modeled against it
  • Bridge restated with every placeholder either confirmed or removed
07

Confirm on Day One

Five items decide whether the Base case holds. All five are one request away.

Colorado rate sheet Medicare A, Managed Care and Medicaid per diems, plus the nursing-component rate per case-mix point. Sets both the skilled lever and the case-mix lever — together up to $1.13M of the Upside case.
Part B collection detail Actual cash collected on 4543-0040, separated from therapy Part B. The 42.2% realization is a blended rate across all Part B lines; respiratory alone may differ, and it moves the exit cost directly.
Payroll and turnover Termination report and current requisitions, to settle 37.4% against 61% and to size how much overtime is structural rather than behavioral.
Respiratory wind-down cost Staffing roster, notice obligations and severance. Excluded from every scenario in this bridge.
A/R aging Medicaid-pending backlog and greater-than-90-day balances behind the $442,677 bad-debt line.
How to read these numbers

Every figure in sections 01 through 05 is computed from the acquisition P&L, the census file, the CO135 listing or the CMS 2567, and reconciles to them. Two inputs are carried from the original model and remain unverified — the $400 rate differential and the 65% contribution margin — and both are flagged where they appear. The case-mix lever is an illustration, not a commitment. Nothing has been benchmarked against outside data or estimated where the documents were silent.
